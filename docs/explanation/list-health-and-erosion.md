# List health & erosion

*Pairs with the **List Health** bookmark. ~2 min read.*

Beneath every send sits the list it draws on, and here is the uncomfortable truth: **open
and click rates only measure whoever is left**. They can look healthy — even improve —
while the audience you can sell to shrinks every month. Campaign metrics will never flag a
dying list. Only the list view does, which is why this bookmark exists.

## The list is a compounding asset

A mailing list was bought once — through acquisition spend or content — and it pays on
every send afterwards, or it decays. Track **net change**: new subscribes minus
unsubscribes minus cleaned addresses. Growing month over month is healthy (a **2–5%
monthly growth rate** is a good band); flat means acquisition only offsets attrition;
sustained negative net change is a future revenue cliff, visible long before revenue
feels it.

## Watch growth quality, not just growth

A list growing faster than your acquisition explains is a warning sign — bought or scraped
contacts predict bounces, complaints, and deliverability damage. Too big too fast is
garbage.

## Localize churn before acting on it

- Unsubscribes spiking on **one send** → that send's content.
- Elevated churn **across all sends** → cadence; you're mailing too often.
- Churn concentrated in **one segment** → sourcing; that audience never wanted this mail.

## What the bookmark reveals

The legend splits net change by mailing list. In the demo data, the main list adds members
every month and the VIP list holds steady — but the legacy blog-signup segment loses
members in all twelve months, with a December cleaning spike where a winback send mostly
harvested bounces. The annotation panel calls it out. A segment like that flatters no
metric: retire it, or win it back deliberately — and check the matrix for each list's
totals over the period.
