# The click: CTR vs CTOR

*Pairs with the **Engagement** bookmark. ~2 min read.*

The mistake most teams make is judging campaigns on opens. An open is the shallowest
signal there is — it only says a message surfaced, and privacy proxies that pre-load
images inflate it badly. Treat open rate as **directional only**: watch its trend, never
its absolute level. **The click is the signal that matters** — it proves the content
matched what the reader wanted and moved them one step toward revenue.

There are two click rates, and they answer different questions.

## CTR — the per-send verdict

**CTR** (clicks ÷ delivered) asks: *of everyone who received this send, how many acted?*
It is a **per-send** metric, judged against the **2–5% band** drawn on the chart. The band
is a per-send number — on a monthly aggregate, sends and their clicks blur across days and
the ratio stops meaning anything. That is why the template shows the band only on the
Individual send axis, and why the report politely objects if you ask for CTR on a monthly
one.

## CTOR — the aggregate's click metric

**CTOR** (clicks ÷ opens) asks: *of those who saw the content, how many acted?* Around
**10%** is average; 14–17% is strong. CTOR is the metric for aggregates and trends,
because it survives the blurring that breaks CTR.

## Read them together

Each weak send then diagnoses itself. In the demo data, the May flash sale opened at
48% but converted only ~2% of those opens — a **copy/CTA problem**: the subject earned the
open and the body squandered it. The March broadcast opened at 12% with a normal CTOR — a
**subject-line problem**: the content was fine, but nobody arrived to see it. Same low
CTR, opposite fixes.

## What the bookmark reveals

In Engagement, drill a month and read each send's CTR against the band, then check its
open rate:

- **High opens + low CTR** → rewrite the body and the call to action.
- **Low opens** → rewrite the subject line.

Never fix both at once, or you won't know which change worked.
